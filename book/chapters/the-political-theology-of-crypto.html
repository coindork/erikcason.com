The Political Theology of Crypto

The oath of machines and the exception that cannot exist

"All significant concepts of the modern theory of the state are secularized theological concepts." -Carl Schmitt, Political Theology

Crypto is a religion insofar that statism is also a religion.

Both rely on belief and faith towards their respective systems to actualize the value of their laws in a meaningful way. Within contemporary legal systems, the value of law is actualized through people who are invested with the authority of law, and the ability to enforce the law with direct legal violence at their own subjective volition -- for better or worse. Within cryptosystems, the rules are enforced by the code alone -- there is no subjective character about it, nor any violence that needs to be applied.

What allows for the operation of both of these systems is the 'oath' that has been taken to consecrate the laws of each system. In legal systems, man is bonded to the law through his oath and obligation to it; within cryptosystems, it is the private key that bonds the code to the outcome. This creates a new system of political power that no longer needs to sullies itself with violence in order to actualize the force of its legal system.

Each system must enforce the rules of their systems precisely to actualize the value of their system of law, or the system must have the possibility of exceptions to any rules; in which case all contemporary law proves its 'situational' nature. As Schmitt says, "For the legal system to make sense a normal situation must exist, and he who is sovereign definitely decides whether this normal situation actually exist."

Contemporary systems of legal power comprises of the oaths of men, who can break their oaths as easily as they are stated. The authority of any and all men has the same weight and power as temporal is their nature; and so it must be the same for the laws of such men. Men have proven over several millennia that they cannot keep their word to the law, and thus shows the very base corruption of any system of law created by the oaths of men and enforcement via violence. This is not the fault of individual men, but is a greater symptom of the nihilism that has caused for the collapse of language in modernity. With the infinite 'exceptions' men of power will always make for themselves and the guile they harbor towards their oaths, the language that bond the oath no longer can substantial the force it once had when it was still a sacrosanct.

However, the oaths of machines are something fundamentally and distinctly different from that of men. Machines cannot lie. They are semantically incapable of it: it is impossible. This then allows for the machines to bond themselves to their oath in a way that man is no longer capable of. It captures and enforces its promise that is contained in the oath with an assurance that no other being can offer. Thus, due to the very nature and being of what language/code is and how it must express itself through the machines, their entire existence as a form of communication at stake and enmeshed in the code/language itself.

For the reason that, "the oath can function as a sacrament of power insofar as it is first of all the sacraments of language" (the scarament of the oath, Giorgio Agamben). For that reason, when the machine makes an oath, commitment, or promise, it fundamentally transforms from simply language into lex. This transformation is what transforms it from merely being into Beyng. It is the transformation that unveils the secret that Bitcoin may very well be a messianic tool for the freedom of all humans everywhere from the yoke and cudgel of fiat money. This is all due to the fact that the force of the oath from the machines substantiates itself in a way that no man, institution, or state can.
